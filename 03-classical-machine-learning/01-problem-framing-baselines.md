# Module 3.1 — Problem Framing and Baselines

## Capability
Convert a vague objective into a measurable ML task and define a baseline before touching sophisticated models.

Ask:
- What decision will prediction influence?
- What is one row/example at prediction time?
- What is the target and when is it known?
- What inputs exist **at prediction time**?
- What errors matter differently?
- What non-ML rule/baseline already exists?

A baseline can be mean/median prediction, majority class, simple business rule, or dummy estimator. If your model cannot beat a meaningful baseline, complexity has not earned its cost.

## Practice
Frame three problems: churn risk, delivery-time regression, fraud review prioritization. For each define target, horizon, unit, baseline, and one reason ML might be inappropriate.

## Mastery
You can write a one-page ML problem statement without naming an algorithm.
