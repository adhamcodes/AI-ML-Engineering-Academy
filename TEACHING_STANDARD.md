# Teaching Standard

Every substantial module should answer:

- What capability is this module building?
- Why does it matter?
- What are the prerequisites?
- What mental model should the learner leave with?
- What terminology must be understood?
- What examples make the idea concrete?
- What mistakes or misconceptions are likely?
- What must the learner do actively?
- How will transfer be tested?
- What counts as mastery?
- What remediation happens after failure?

## Preferred learning loop

**Learn → Explain → Predict/Trace → Practice → Debug → Build → Transfer → Assess → Repair if needed**

A short concept does not need a giant template. Structure is a quality contract, not a formatting prison.

## Scaffolding

Early modules may provide guided steps and starter code. Later modules should increasingly provide specifications, failing systems, incomplete documentation, unfamiliar code, and engineering constraints instead of recipes.

## AI usage

- Learning: AI may explain concepts unless a gate says otherwise.
- Core practice: solution generation should be restricted when the exercise is intended to prove independent skill.
- Assessments: default to closed-solution assistance unless explicitly marked open-tool.
- Professional simulation: AI tools may be allowed, but the learner owns correctness, tests, security, and explanation.
